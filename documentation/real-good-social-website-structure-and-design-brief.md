# Real Good Social Website Structure and Design Brief

**Domain:** [realgoodsocial.org](https://realgoodsocial.org)  
**Organisation:** Real Good Social  
**Stage:** Early-stage social enterprise

## 1. Purpose of the Website

The website should establish Real Good Social as a credible early-stage social enterprise, clearly explain its purpose and areas of work, and create appropriate pathways for potential partners, contributors, supporters, and members of the public.

It should communicate that Real Good Social develops practical ventures, technologies, partnerships, and organisational systems that help people and institutions produce durable social good.

The site should:

- explain what Real Good Social is;
- communicate a coherent but appropriately scoped organisational vision;
- present selected initiatives without overstating their maturity;
- invite relevant forms of partnership and participation;
- demonstrate intellectual and operational credibility;
- provide a durable public foundation as the venture develops.

## 2. Core Positioning

### Primary proposition

> **Building practical systems for social good.**

Real Good Social develops ventures, technologies, and partnerships that help people and organisations translate good intentions into effective, durable action.

### Supporting message

Many worthwhile efforts are constrained by fragmented systems, limited coordination, weak institutional pathways, or insufficient practical support. Real Good Social works to develop the ventures, tools, and collaborations needed to overcome these barriers.

### Brand and legal architecture

| Name | Role | Use when referring to |
|---|---|---|
| **Real Good** | friendly, abbreviated public-facing identity | the logo, wordmark and conversational brand expression |
| **Real Good Social** | the social enterprise and fuller brand identity | the enterprise itself, in all ordinary descriptive copy |
| **Real Good Ventures Pty Ltd** | the legal entity operating Real Good Social | legal responsibility, liability, ownership, privacy and contractual matters |

- Real Good is not a parent or master brand above Real Good Social. Avoid "a Real Good™ social enterprise", "part of the Real Good™ family", "Real Good™ ecosystem" and "a Real Good venture".
- Real Good Social should read as a substantive social enterprise, not as a registered business name, division or initiative of Real Good Ventures. Business-name terminology belongs only on legal pages where necessary.
- Keep the corporate entity in the background; show it only where useful or legally appropriate.
- Do not use ™ in ordinary website prose. Reserve it for the logo, brand guidelines, formal brand documentation and trade mark notices. Do not use ® until the mark is registered.
- Do not describe the structure as the "Real Good Australia group".

## 3. Recommended Site Structure

### 3.1 Home

The homepage should quickly answer four questions:

1. What is Real Good Social?
2. What problems does it address?
3. What is it currently developing?
4. How can someone engage?

#### Recommended homepage sections

1. **Hero**
   - Primary proposition
   - Short organisational description
   - Primary call to action: **Explore our work**
   - Secondary call to action: **Partner with us**

2. **What Real Good Social does**
   - Social ventures
   - Technology for good
   - Partnerships for good
   - Strategy and systems

3. **Why Real Good Social exists**
   - A concise description of the gap between good intentions and effective action

4. **Featured initiatives**
   - Two to four selected projects
   - Visible development-stage labels

5. **How we work**
   - Understand the problem
   - Develop a practical response
   - Build with relevant partners
   - Test, learn, and improve
   - Establish durable capability

6. **Who we work with**
   - Community organisations
   - Institutions
   - Researchers
   - Builders and specialists
   - Supporters

7. **Latest insight or progress update**

8. **Final engagement call**
   - Propose a partnership
   - Contribute expertise
   - Follow the organisation's progress

The homepage should present a coherent direction without making Real Good Social appear more mature, extensive, or operationally established than it currently is.

---

### 3.2 About

The About page should explain the organisation in durable terms.

#### Our purpose

To make effective social action easier to initiate, coordinate, sustain, and scale.

#### Our approach

Real Good Social combines:

- social venture development;
- technology for good;
- partnerships and institutional collaboration;
- knowledge, strategy, and practical implementation.

#### Why Real Good Social exists

Many people and organisations want to produce meaningful social good but lack the systems, tools, relationships, resources, or operating structures required to turn concern into sustained action.

Real Good Social exists to help bridge this gap.

#### Principles

- Practical good over symbolic good
- Human agency and dignity
- Evidence, reflection, and continuous improvement
- Responsible technology
- Partnership rather than unnecessary duplication
- Long-term social value
- Honesty about uncertainty and development stage

#### Founder section

A concise founder section may explain the origin and direction of Real Good Social while maintaining the organisation as the primary subject.

It should establish accountability and intent without making the site primarily a personal profile.

---

### 3.3 What We Do

The organisation's work should be grouped into a small number of public-facing areas.

#### Social Ventures

Real Good Social develops and supports ventures addressing social, cultural, environmental, and human needs.

#### Technology for Good

Real Good Social designs digital tools and infrastructure that improve agency, access, coordination, knowledge, accountability, and participation.

#### Partnerships for Good

Real Good Social works with community organisations, researchers, institutions, practitioners, and other aligned organisations to develop and implement practical responses.

#### Strategy and Systems

Real Good Social examines how promising ideas can become sustainable operating models, organisational capabilities, services, and ventures.

These categories are broad enough to remain useful as the portfolio evolves while remaining understandable to external audiences.

---

### 3.4 Initiatives

The Initiatives page should present only projects that are sufficiently developed to communicate publicly.

Each initiative profile should include:

- the problem or opportunity;
- the proposed response;
- the current development stage;
- intended beneficiaries;
- current activities;
- collaborators or support sought;
- the next meaningful milestone.

#### Recommended initiative status labels

- **Exploring**
- **Researching**
- **Designing**
- **Prototyping**
- **Piloting**
- **Operating**

These labels should appear consistently throughout the site.

An initial public portfolio of three to five initiatives is sufficient. The wider body of Real Good Social's work should remain simplified until its internal relationships can be explained clearly to an external audience.

---

### 3.5 Partner With Us

This page should address distinct audiences and provide concrete engagement pathways.

#### Community organisations

Collaborate on a problem, pilot a tool, strengthen an existing service, or develop a new response.

#### Institutions

Explore partnerships involving research, technology, programme design, social innovation, service development, or implementation.

#### Researchers and specialists

Contribute domain knowledge, evidence, evaluation, design expertise, or technical capability.

#### Builders and contributors

Participate in product development, design, operations, communications, research, or venture formation.

#### Supporters and funders

Provide introductions, sponsorship, philanthropic support, resources, strategic advice, or support for a specific initiative.

#### Recommended calls to action

- Propose a partnership
- Share a problem or opportunity
- Contribute expertise
- Support an initiative
- Arrange an introductory conversation

Early-stage credibility is strengthened when the organisation clearly identifies the kinds of engagement it is prepared to undertake.

---

### 3.6 Insights

The Insights section should function as a restrained publication area rather than a high-frequency blog.

#### Content types

- Project notes
- Research summaries
- Social venture concepts
- Design principles
- Progress updates
- Reflections on technology and social good
- Partnership learnings

#### Suggested categories

- **Ideas**
- **Projects**
- **Research**
- **Updates**

A small number of substantial and well-developed pieces is preferable to frequent low-value publishing.

---

### 3.7 Contact

The Contact page should include:

- a general enquiry form;
- a partnership enquiry pathway;
- a contributor expression-of-interest pathway;
- an organisational email address;
- location: Canberra, Australia;
- legal details, presented separately from marketing copy: legal entity (Real Good Ventures Pty Ltd), operating brand (Real Good Social) and ABN.

The enquiry form should allow users to identify the purpose of their contact.

Suggested enquiry categories:

- Partnership
- Project or initiative
- Research collaboration
- Contributing expertise
- Funding or support
- Media
- General enquiry

Separate departmental email addresses are unnecessary at this stage.

## 6. Design Brief

### 6.1 Brand Character

The website should feel:

- principled;
- intelligent;
- constructive;
- humane;
- contemporary;
- quietly ambitious;
- operationally credible;
- optimistic without exaggeration.

It should not resemble:

- a conventional charity donation website;
- a venture-capital startup making inflated claims;
- a government consultancy;
- an abstract philosophical manifesto;
- a collection of unrelated social projects.

The central design tension is:

> **Moral seriousness combined with practical optimism.**

---

### 6.2 Design Language

The site should use a clean editorial and institutional design system with restrained warmth.

#### Recommended characteristics

- generous whitespace;
- strong typographic hierarchy;
- short, readable blocks of copy;
- clear page structure;
- modular initiative cards;
- subtle lines, grids, and connective elements;
- restrained animation;
- limited and purposeful use of illustration;
- documentary or human-centred imagery;
- visible status and stage indicators;
- high accessibility and strong contrast.

The design should suggest that Real Good Social is an organising institution and venture platform for social action rather than a single campaign.

---

### 6.3 Visual Motifs

Potential recurring motifs include:

- connected points and pathways;
- expanding circles;
- structured fields and grids;
- layered systems;
- bridges between ideas and action;
- subtle horizon lines;
- gradual upward or outward movement.

These motifs should remain abstract and restrained.

Avoid generic social-impact imagery such as:

- hands forming hearts;
- globes held in palms;
- saplings;
- staged volunteering;
- anonymous corporate handshakes;
- exaggerated utopian imagery.

---

## 7. Colour System

### 7.1 Primary Palette

#### Real Good Blue

`#2457D6`

Use for:

- primary calls to action;
- links;
- key brand elements;
- selected highlights;
- focus states.

It communicates trust, clarity, capability, and constructive action.

#### Deep Ink

`#14213D`

Use for:

- headings;
- navigation;
- dark sections;
- high-emphasis text.

It provides seriousness without relying on pure black.

#### Warm White

`#F8F7F3`

Use as the principal background colour.

It creates a more humane and considered atmosphere than stark white.

## 8. Typography

Use a humanist sans-serif for the main interface and an optional editorial serif for selected statements.

### Recommended pairings

- Inter and Source Serif 4
- Manrope and Newsreader
- Public Sans and Literata

### Suggested use

#### Sans-serif

Use for:

- navigation;
- body copy;
- labels;
- buttons;
- forms;
- interface components.

#### Serif

Use selectively for:

- major principles;
- quotations;
- section introductions;
- high-level statements.

Typography should communicate clarity, public purpose, and thoughtfulness rather than technological novelty.

## 9. Imagery

Prioritise:

- real people engaged in meaningful work;
- communities, researchers, and builders in context;
- prototypes and project artefacts;
- workshops and collaborative processes;
- places and cultural environments relevant to initiatives;
- detailed images showing care, thought, and practical activity.

Avoid:

- generic corporate stock photography;
- anonymous boardroom imagery;
- poverty imagery used primarily to evoke guilt;
- excessive AI-generated utopian imagery;
- images implying programmes or operations that do not yet exist.

At this stage, diagrams, prototypes, working materials, and project artefacts may provide greater credibility than highly polished lifestyle photography.

## 10. Voice and Tone

The written voice should be:

- direct;
- measured;
- hopeful;
- specific;
- non-performative;
- intellectually serious;
- accessible;
- transparent about development stage.

### Preferred style

> We develop practical ventures and systems that help people organise effective social action.

### Avoid

> We are revolutionising the future of global impact through transformative innovation.

### Stage transparency

Use clear language such as:

> This initiative is currently being researched.

> We are developing an initial prototype.

> We are seeking community and research partners for a pilot.

> This concept is at an early exploratory stage.

Transparency about uncertainty and maturity should be treated as part of the Real Good Social brand.

## 11. Interaction and Interface Principles

The site should:

- make primary actions obvious;
- minimise unnecessary navigation depth;
- keep forms short and purposeful;
- use plain-language labels;
- clearly distinguish active initiatives from exploratory concepts;
- provide visible feedback after form submission;
- work effectively on mobile devices;
- meet WCAG 2.2 AA accessibility expectations;
- avoid animation that interferes with reading or navigation.

Buttons should use specific verbs, such as:

- Explore our work
- View the initiative
- Propose a partnership
- Contribute expertise
- Read the project note
- Contact Real Good Social

Avoid vague calls to action such as **Learn more** where a more specific label is possible.

## 12. Recommended Launch Scope

A credible first release should include:

1. One strong homepage
2. About page
3. What We Do page
4. Three initiative profiles
5. Partner With Us page
6. Contact page
7. One foundational insight article
8. Privacy page
9. Terms page
10. Basic accessibility statement

This is sufficient to establish Real Good Social publicly while allowing the organisation, portfolio, and legal structure to mature.

## 13. Content Priorities Before Launch

Before launch, prepare:

- a concise organisational description;
- a clear purpose statement;
- a founder biography;
- three public initiative summaries;
- one substantial foundational article;
- partnership categories;
- a general contact pathway;
- accurate legal and registration details;
- a small set of authentic visual assets;
- privacy and terms content.

## 14. Success Criteria

The initial website should succeed when a visitor can understand, within a few minutes:

- what Real Good Social is;
- why it exists;
- what kinds of work it undertakes;
- which initiatives are currently active;
- how mature those initiatives are;
- whether the organisation is relevant to them;
- how they can engage.

The website should leave visitors with the impression that Real Good Social is thoughtful, credible, practically oriented, honest about its current stage, and capable of becoming a durable institution for social good.
